## General meaning
Arcana 13, Death, is probably the most feared and the most misunderstood within the Matriz del Destino, though its meaning has little to do with a literal ending. It represents the definitive closing of cycles, those deep transformations that demand leaving behind an earlier version of oneself in order to continue. Those who carry this arcana present in their matrix tend to live through several "symbolic deaths" over the course of their life: relationships, work stages, or ways of thinking that end abruptly to make way for something new.

This energy doesn't ask whether you're ready — it simply happens, which is why it generates so much resistance at first. However, those who learn to work with it discover that every closure, however painful it may seem, clears the way for a genuine rebirth.

## Positive and negative expressions
Lived in a positive way, Death becomes an almost innate ability to reinvent oneself as many times as needed. There's no rigid attachment to the known, but rather the trust that every ending opens space for something better. This flexibility in the face of change tends to be one of the greatest strengths of those who carry this arcana.

On its darker side, though, a paralysing fear of change appears: clinging to bonds, jobs, or beliefs that have already run their course, purely out of fear of the unknown. This resistance doesn't stop the inevitable — it only makes it more painful, unnecessarily prolonging processes that should already have closed.

## In love
In relationships, this arcana speaks to the ability, or the difficulty, to let go of bonds that no longer bring growth. Those who carry it active in their matrix tend to face relationships that end sharply, with no room for half-measures, and learn over time that closing those chapters with honesty is an act of self-love.

## At work
Professionally, Death translates into a willingness to reinvent oneself when a work stage comes to an end, even if that means changing direction completely. Ignoring this signal tends to result in stagnation; accepting it, on the other hand, opens doors that previously seemed closed.
